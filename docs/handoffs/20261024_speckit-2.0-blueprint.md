# Handoff: SpecKit 2.0 vision: a reference spectral-inference library

## Scope

This note is the north-star roadmap above
`docs/handoffs/20261024_ltpda-feature-gaps.md`.

The ten feature handoffs remain useful implementation notes. They are not a
sufficient product or research strategy. Implementing all ten would make
SpecKit more convenient and closer to LTPDA, but it would not by itself make
SpecKit a field-leading spectral library.

This handoff asks a different question:

> What should SpecKit make possible, and what should it prove, that neither
> LTPDA nor ordinary Welch wrappers make possible?

No implementation is part of this note.

## Current status

Strategic design only. No code has been added for this roadmap.

The existing code already has an unusually strong starting point:

- dynamic per-frequency segment lengths and starts;
- arbitrary non-integer frequencies through Goertzel kernels;
- one-sided PSD, CSD, transfer-function, coherence, and conditioned spectra;
- the same statistic on Numba and NumPy paths;
- per-bin overlap-corrected degrees of freedom;
- explicit one-sided estimator versus two-sided generator conventions;
- a focused dependency set and a public paper in preparation.

Several foundations are still weaker than the public scientific claim:

- The default `vectorized_ltf_plan` is not covered by
  `tests/unit/test_schedulers.py`.
- The scheduler is a useful heuristic, not an optimizer with a stated loss.
- The result reports per-bin uncertainty but not estimator bias or
  cross-frequency covariance.
- `nu` is based on a locally white Gaussian approximation and ignores the
  detrend, DC/Nyquist pseudo-covariance, and spectrum variation across the
  window bandwidth.
- `M2` is observed segment scatter, but converting it to uncertainty of the
  mean still uses the same overlap model. It is therefore not an
  assumption-free uncertainty estimate.
- Non-finite input samples are silently replaced by zero. That is a spectral
  operation with a window of its own, not harmless sanitation.
- The default result does not preserve a compact, serializable record of the
  exact estimator operator.
- Existing accuracy tests use useful but broad aggregate tolerances. They do
  not yet constitute a metrology-grade validation corpus.

The ten feature handoffs address some of these points, especially degrees of
freedom and the N-channel matrix. They mostly add operations around the
estimator rather than deepen the estimator itself.

## Executive decision

Do not organize the next major release around LTPDA feature parity.

Organize it around one research-grade promise:

> For every reported spectral quantity, SpecKit can state and test the exact
> finite-record measurement operator, its resolution and leakage, its
> statistical information, its assumptions, and the data decisions that
> produced it.

The distinctive product should be an **auditable dynamic-resolution spectral
inference engine**, not a collection of spectral utility functions.

“Ground-breaking” must be earned by at least one demonstrated estimator
advance:

1. a dynamic-resolution scheduler that improves a predeclared bias/variance
   or mean-square-error objective at fixed data and compute budget;
2. a debiased dynamic-resolution WOSA estimator whose improvement is measured
   against ordinary LTF/LPSD and Welch;
3. a dynamic-resolution multitaper estimator with valid finite-sample
   uncertainty and line tests; or
4. a multichannel dynamic-resolution spectral-matrix estimator with stable
   conditional inference and calibrated uncertainty.

GPU execution, more methods, and LTPDA parity do not meet that bar.

## What a top professional would do beyond the handoffs

### Start with the estimand, not the API

They would first decide exactly what is being estimated:

- the spectrum of a zero-mean, wide-sense stationary process;
- a local spectrum of a slowly varying process;
- the spectral matrix of a multivariate process;
- a transfer function under a stated input/output noise model;
- or a finite-record descriptive transform.

Those are different statistical objects. A single `compute_spectrum` call
must not slide between them without saying so.

They would state the observation model too:

- uniformly sampled and complete;
- uniformly sampled with a validity mask;
- irregularly sampled;
- quantized, clipped, or saturated;
- calibrated through an instrument response;
- or contaminated by deterministic lines, drift, and transient glitches.

The handoffs mostly begin after these choices have already been made.

### Derive the complete finite-record operator

For bin `j`, segment `k`, window `w_j`, start `D_jk`, detrend projection
`Q_j Q_j.T`, and target frequency `f_j`, the estimator is a quadratic form in
the samples. A professional treatment would make that operator explicit.

For a stationary process with true spectrum `S`, the expected estimate has
the form

```
E[S_hat_j] = integral K_j(nu) S(nu) dnu
```

where `K_j` includes:

- the actual segment length;
- the exact window samples;
- the non-integer target frequency;
- the one-sided folding rule;
- the detrend projection;
- segment placement;
- and, for a cross-spectrum, the relative timing and channel convention.

`ENBW` is only one scalar summary of `K_j`. It does not describe the sidelobe
structure, low-frequency folding, line leakage, or bias on a sloped
continuum.

The same derivation should expose

```
Cov(S_hat_j, S_hat_l)
```

because neighboring dynamic bins reuse samples and have overlapping spectral
windows. Per-bin error bars do not imply independent bins. Continuum fits,
line searches, and integrated quantities need this distinction.

### Treat bias and variance together

The current public story is strongest on variance. A professional would not
call an estimate more certain merely because more short segments reduced its
variance while its leakage bias increased.

The scheduler should therefore be judged on risk, not on `Jdes` and `Kdes`
alone. Candidate objectives include:

```
risk_j = predicted_bias_j**2 + predicted_variance_j
```

or a minimax bound over a declared class of spectra and lines. The objective
may include constraints on:

- relative bandwidth `r_j / f_j`;
- minimum resolvable line spacing;
- maximum leakage from an out-of-band tone;
- delay bias;
- effective degrees of freedom;
- total computation;
- and maximum memory.

The existing LTF recurrence becomes a baseline and a fast feasible solution,
not an unquestioned definition of the best plan.

### Separate exploratory diagnostics from confirmatory inference

A plotted spectrum, a detected line, and a confidence statement are different
outputs.

A professional library would distinguish:

- pointwise intervals from simultaneous bands;
- exploratory peak flags from line-detection p-values;
- nominal degrees of freedom from empirically calibrated coverage;
- a fitted continuum from a window-convolved expected spectrum;
- and observed frequencies from an interpolated display curve.

This matters more than adding another plotting helper.

### Preserve a minimal scientific audit trail

The feature-gap summary declares LTPDA-style provenance out of scope. A full
LTPDA history graph would indeed be too heavy, but no provenance at all is
not appropriate for a reference scientific estimator.

SpecKit needs a small immutable record containing:

- package and schema version;
- input shape, sample rate, and time origin when supplied;
- input validity policy and count of rejected samples;
- exact plan and window identity;
- normalization and PSD convention;
- detrend order;
- kernel backend and floating dtype;
- optional channel names and units;
- and a stable hash of the plan and configuration.

This is not a workflow database. It is enough information to reproduce the
numbers and identify incompatible results.

### Validate the scientific claim independently

LTPDA is evidence, not an oracle. Its raw tree contains valuable algorithms,
but also divergent CSD conventions, approximate degrees of freedom, rounded
shifts, duplicated implementations, and weak executable tests.

A top professional would require independent agreement among:

1. a small direct-DFT reference written for clarity;
2. analytic cases;
3. Monte Carlo coverage;
4. SciPy where the estimators are genuinely equivalent;
5. selected LTPDA cases, with the exact path and convention recorded;
6. and real instrument records with known injected or calibrated behavior.

They would also ask an independent domain expert to review the mathematical
contract before optimizing the implementation.

## The rawest material to start from

The existing handoffs are interpretations. Start one layer below them.

### Physical questions and real records

Begin with three to five representative scientific decisions, not a method
catalog. Examples:

- Is the high-frequency floor of a GRACE-FO LRI record physical or window
  leakage?
- What transfer function and delay are consistent with a calibrated injected
  disturbance?
- Which environmental channels explain an output after conditioning on
  correlated witnesses?
- Is a narrow feature a coherent line, a transient, or a continuum
  fluctuation?
- How does a long gap alter the evidence for a low-frequency feature?

For each case, preserve the raw samples, validity mask, calibration metadata,
and expected scientific conclusion. These become acceptance datasets.

### Primary mathematics

Read and derive from the primary sources rather than from LTPDA wrappers:

- Welch (1967) for overlapped segmented averaging.
- Tröbs and Heinzel (2006) for logarithmic PSD scheduling.
- Heinzel, Rüdiger, and Schilling (2002) for window normalization, ENBW, and
  leakage.
- Percival and Walden (1993) for overlap covariance and spectral estimation.
- Bendat and Piersol for auto/cross-spectral sampling distributions, transfer
  functions, and conditioned spectra.
- Seybert and Hamilton (1978) for delay-induced FRF and coherence bias.
- Thomson (1982) for multitaper estimation, adaptive weighting, line tests,
  and the bias/resolution problem.
- Thomson (2007) for jackknife uncertainty on multitaper spectra and
  coherence.
- Astfalck, Sykulski, and Cripps (2024) for debiasing Welch estimates by
  fitting the expected blurred basis.

The output of this reading should be derivations and executable reference
calculations, not prose summaries alone.

### Raw implementations

Use these LTPDA files directly when establishing behavior:

- `ltpda/classes/@ao/ltf_plan.m`
- `ltpda/classes/@ao/mlpsd_m.m`
- `ltpda/classes/@ao/mltfe.m`
- `ltpda/classes/@ao/wosa.m`
- `ltpda/src/ltpda_dft/`
- `ltpda/classes/@ao/getdof.m`
- `ltpda/classes/@ao/psdconf.m`
- `ltpda/classes/@ao/confint.m`
- `ltpda/classes/+utils/@math/overlapCorr.m`
- the individual feature implementations named by the existing handoffs

Read the C/MEX core where available. The MATLAB object wrappers often hide
the actual segment statistic, normalization, and rounding rule.

The script `ltpda/src/ltpda_dft/test_ltpda_lpsd_new.m` is useful historical
evidence, but it is a visual old/new comparison rather than a numerical
oracle. Do not promote it into a gold standard.

### Raw SpecKit behavior

Treat these as the current executable specification:

- `speckit/core.py`
- `speckit/schedulers.py`
- `speckit/analysis.py`
- `speckit/flattop.py`
- `tests/unit/test_core.py`
- `tests/unit/test_schedulers.py`
- `tests/integration/test_analysis_api.py`
- `tests/integration/test_accuracy.py`
- `tests/integration/test_errors.py`

Record outputs from released versions before changing an estimator. A seeded
array is regression evidence, not proof of correctness.

### Independent numerical oracle

Build a deliberately slow reference evaluator that:

- constructs each raw segment explicitly;
- applies the detrend as a matrix projection;
- applies the exact window;
- computes `sum x[n] exp(-i omega n)` directly;
- retains each segment/taper Fourier coefficient;
- forms autospectra, cross-spectra, and the full spectral matrix;
- and applies one-sided scaling only after the two-sided statistic is clear.

This oracle must not call the production Goertzel kernel or scheduler.
Production performance work is checked against it.

## North-star scientific architecture

### 1. A first-class spectral plan

Replace “a dict the scheduler happened to return” as the conceptual contract
with a typed, immutable, serializable `SpectralPlan`.

It should contain or identify:

- `f`, `r`, `b`, `L`, and exact starts `D`;
- the exact window construction and normalization;
- the detrend operator;
- one-sided/two-sided and CSD orientation conventions;
- expected segment and taper counts;
- per-bin `nu` and the approximation used to obtain it;
- estimated compute and memory cost;
- a plan schema version and stable hash.

The current dict can remain as a compatibility view. The plan object should
validate invariants before any kernel runs.

Add a `plan.report()` diagnostic that makes boundary regimes, achieved
overlap, expected resolution, information, and warnings visible. It should
report facts, not silently retune the plan.

### 2. An explicit response operator

Add a reference-level way to inspect the spectral response of each bin:

- main-lobe width;
- ENBW;
- coherent gain;
- sidelobe envelope or selected leakage levels;
- negative-frequency folding;
- response after polynomial detrending;
- and response to a pure delay in cross mode.

The initial implementation may evaluate the operator numerically. It does not
need to run in every production call.

This operator becomes the basis for:

- choosing `bmin`;
- diagnosing low-frequency bias;
- predicting leakage from lines;
- fitting a continuum through the estimator rather than through raw points;
- and designing a scheduler objective.

### 3. Replicates as an optional result level

The five-field `BinStats` record is efficient for present two-channel
quantities. It is too compressed for jackknife inference, robust averaging,
multitaper adaptation, full covariance, and many multichannel diagnostics.

Keep the summary path as the default. Add an explicit result level:

- `storage="summary"` for current sufficient statistics;
- `storage="replicates"` for segment/taper Fourier coefficients or
  periodogram matrices;
- optionally a streaming callback for out-of-core consumers.

Do not make all users pay the memory cost. Do not invent separate kernels for
every downstream feature; define one replicate representation and reduce it
in tested ways.

### 4. Estimator families sharing one contract

Support a small number of estimator families behind the same plan/result
contract.

**Dynamic WOSA** is the current baseline.

**Debiased dynamic WOSA** should adapt the Astfalck et al. idea to
frequency-dependent kernels. Fit basis spectra after passing each basis
through the actual SpecKit response, then recover the unblurred coefficients.
This is research work: first establish positivity, conditioning, covariance,
and behavior near boundaries.

**Dynamic multitaper** should use DPSS tapers appropriate to each segment
length and target bandwidth, with adaptive weighting where justified.
Orthogonal tapers provide replicate information without pretending that
heavily overlapped time segments are independent. The combination of time
overlap and tapers needs a derived effective covariance; do not add their
degrees of freedom by inspection.

**Robust dynamic spectra** should address glitches and heavy tails with a
declared robust reduction, such as a calibrated trimmed or median-of-means
estimator. A median periodogram is biased unless corrected. Robustness must be
tested on both clean Gaussian data and contaminated data.

Only estimators that beat the baseline on a predeclared benchmark should
become public.

### 5. A real multichannel spectral-matrix core

The N-channel handoff is foundational, but the target should be more than all
pairs on one grid.

At each frequency, estimate the Hermitian matrix

```
S_j = mean_k z_jk z_jk.H
```

from one shared set of channel Fourier vectors `z_jk`.

This construction gives a positive-semidefinite matrix by design. From it,
derive:

- auto- and cross-spectra;
- ordinary and multiple coherence;
- partial coherence from a regularized precision matrix;
- optimal MISO residuals;
- coherent/incoherent noise budgets;
- matrix whitening;
- and uncertainty that respects shared replicates.

When channel count approaches effective replicate count, inversion must be a
statistical operation, not just `pinv` after a condition-number threshold.
Evaluate positive-definite shrinkage and report the regularization strength.

Pairwise `compute_spectrum` remains a compatibility path. It must become a
view of the matrix statistic, not a separate scientific definition.

### 6. Missingness as part of the observation model

Before adding gap filling, replace silent zero substitution with an explicit
policy:

- `missing="raise"` as the scientific default;
- an explicit validity mask;
- named interpolation or conditional reconstruction;
- and a missing-data spectral estimator when the user wants a spectrum rather
  than a completed time series.

Gap filling and spectral estimation are different goals. A filled realization
must carry reconstruction uncertainty and must not be presented as observed
data.

Evaluate the 2024 Damaschke, Kühn, and Nobach covariance/PSD estimator for
uniformly sampled data with missing samples. It explicitly avoids
interpolation and block subdivision. Compare it with LTPDA's PSD-weighted
conditional fill.

Gaussian-process spectral inference is a possible optional research backend
for irregular sampling and long gaps. It should not become a required
dependency or be called LPSD.

### 7. Stationarity and time localization

Every averaged spectrum assumes enough stationarity for the replicates to
represent one process. Add diagnostics before adding an elaborate
spectrogram:

- per-segment power and cross-power traces;
- change-point or consistency flags across replicates;
- robust outlier scores;
- and a comparison of early/late or blocked estimates.

Then define a dynamic-resolution time-frequency result whose time and
frequency resolution are explicit. A standard SciPy `ShortTimeFFT`
spectrogram is a useful fixed-resolution interoperability baseline, but it is
not SpecKit's research contribution.

### 8. Inference-aware model fitting

Do not make log-polynomial ordinary least squares the main fitting story.
Log-transforming a noisy PSD changes the error distribution, and adjacent
dynamic bins are correlated.

The production fitting interface should be able to fit:

- the expected window-convolved model;
- with per-bin effective degrees of freedom;
- with optional cross-frequency covariance or a documented diagonal
  approximation;
- while separating continuum and deterministic lines;
- and while returning parameter covariance or posterior uncertainty.

A simple log-polynomial fit can still exist as an exploratory utility. It
must be labeled accordingly.

Transfer-function fitting should state the noise model. The H1, H2, and
errors-in-variables estimators answer different questions. A generic complex
pole-residue fit to `Hxy` is not automatically unbiased.

### 9. Interoperability without surrendering the contract

Keep NumPy arrays as the core representation. Add optional adapters rather
than hard dependencies:

- SciPy `welch`, `csd`, and `ShortTimeFFT` comparison/conversion;
- xarray for labeled channel/frequency matrices;
- Astropy or Pint quantities when installed;
- GWpy `TimeSeries` / `FrequencySeries` adapters;
- and export of plan/result metadata to JSON plus arrays to a stable binary
  format.

Unit strings are an acceptable short-term step. They are not the final answer
for cross-channel units, transfer functions, calibration, or arithmetic.

### 10. Performance follows the estimator design

Benchmark end-to-end cost as:

- planning;
- window/detrend preparation;
- transform/reduction;
- uncertainty/response calculations;
- peak memory;
- and first-call versus warmed JIT time.

Optimize repeated segment lengths in batches and reuse transforms where the
plan allows it. Add streaming/out-of-core execution before assuming the
answer is a GPU.

CUDA is worthwhile only after:

- the reference operator exists;
- CPU results are stable;
- transfer and compilation costs are included;
- and a realistic large-data benchmark shows a user-visible win.

## Relationship to the ten LTPDA feature handoffs

Keep all ten handoffs, but change their role and order.

### Promote to foundations

1. **Overlap-corrected uncertainty.** Finish the pointwise PSD interval, then
   expand the work into coverage, bias, and cross-frequency dependence.

2. **Shared-plan N-channel matrix.** Redesign it around shared channel Fourier
   vectors and a matrix result, not a larger `BinStats` tuple alone.

3. **Units.** Add short-term metadata, but design it as part of the result
   schema and optional quantity adapters.

### Fold into deeper estimator work

4. **Color and whiten.** Use spectrum synthesis as a validation instrument.
   Matrix whitening follows the matrix estimator and regularization work.

5. **PSD-weighted gap filling.** Put it behind an explicit missing-data
   contract and compare it with direct missing-data spectral estimation.

6. **Coherent line subtraction.** Replace “fit peaks then subtract” as the
   north star with joint line/continuum inference. A time-domain sine fit
   remains a useful utility.

7. **Continuum and transfer-function fits.** Fit the expected measured
   spectrum and state the transfer-function noise model. Keep log-polynomial
   OLS exploratory.

### Retain as useful, non-differentiating utilities

8. **Delay estimate.** Valuable and testable, but not a strategic pillar.

9. **Component noise projection.** Useful after the spectral matrix and unit
   contracts. Support coherent cross-terms rather than stopping permanently
   at incoherent inputs.

10. **Logarithmic rebinning.** Useful interoperability for Welch outputs. It
    is not an estimator advance and should not be confused with native LPSD.

## Validation program

### Contract tests

Lock, in executable tests:

- one-sided versus two-sided density at interior, DC, and Nyquist cases;
- CSD orientation and physical transfer-function phase;
- exact detrend and window order;
- segment-start rounding;
- the behavior of every missing-data policy;
- units for auto, cross, and transfer quantities;
- and plan serialization round trips.

No non-finite kernel output should be changed to zero without a recorded,
tested policy.

### Direct-transform tests

For every detrend order, window family, and representative fractional bin:

- compare each production Fourier coefficient or summary to the independent
  direct DFT;
- compare Numba and NumPy;
- test both one and multiple channels;
- include very short and very long segments;
- and include frequencies near zero and Nyquist.

The tolerance should come from a floating-point error analysis or measured
roundoff scaling, not from what makes one fixture pass.

### Statistical calibration

Use seeded Monte Carlo families, not one fixture:

- white and colored Gaussian spectra;
- power laws with several slopes;
- narrow lines at on-bin and off-bin frequencies;
- known filters and delays;
- controlled coherence;
- correlated multichannel mixtures;
- heavy-tailed and transient contamination;
- random missing samples and long gaps;
- and slowly nonstationary records.

Check:

- bias;
- variance;
- pointwise interval coverage;
- simultaneous coverage when claimed;
- false line-detection rate and power;
- transfer-function magnitude and phase;
- matrix eigenvalues and condition;
- and parameter-fit coverage.

Publish the seeds, sample counts, process definitions, and expected analytic
quantities.

### Quality-versus-cost benchmarks

Compare at matched data length and compute or memory budget:

- SciPy Welch;
- current SpecKit LTF/LPSD;
- LTPDA where reproducible;
- multitaper;
- debiased Welch;
- and each proposed SpecKit estimator.

Measure scientific loss as well as wall time:

- integrated squared error on linear and log PSD scales;
- worst-band relative bias;
- line leakage into a declared quiet band;
- effective resolution;
- interval calibration;
- transfer-function error;
- and spectral-matrix conditioning.

A new scheduler or estimator lands only if the benchmark states where it
wins, where it loses, and why.

### Real-data acceptance cases

Keep small redistributable records or scripts that retrieve immutable public
records. Include at least:

- a high-dynamic-range leakage case;
- a known-delay cross-spectral case;
- a multichannel subtraction case;
- a line-plus-continuum case;
- and a gap/nonstationarity case.

Synthetic success alone is insufficient for the public “precision
measurement” claim.

## Success criteria

This roadmap succeeds when all of the following are true.

### Scientific definition

- Every public estimator names its estimand, stationarity assumptions, PSD
  convention, CSD convention, missing-data policy, and normalization.
- Every result can expose the exact plan and enough metadata to reproduce it.
- The response of a requested bin to a sinusoid or model spectrum can be
  evaluated independently of the production kernel.

### Correctness

- The production kernels agree with a direct-DFT oracle across the declared
  domain.
- Numba and NumPy compute the same statistics.
- Pointwise intervals achieve nominal coverage within predeclared Monte Carlo
  uncertainty on the process classes for which they are documented.
- Spectral matrices are Hermitian positive semidefinite by construction;
  regularized inverses report their regularization.
- Missing samples are never silently interpreted as physical zeros.

### Research contribution

- At least one new dynamic-resolution estimator or scheduler beats the
  current LTF baseline on a preregistered quality-versus-cost benchmark.
- The improvement is explained by the response and covariance model, not by a
  post-hoc scale factor or selected plot.
- The method, failure modes, benchmark corpus, and implementation are
  publishable and reproducible.

### Software quality

- The default scheduler is directly tested.
- Plans and result metadata have versioned serialization.
- Large inputs have a bounded-memory path.
- Optional integrations remain optional.
- Public compatibility is preserved or changed through a documented major
  version.

## Recommended next steps

### Phase 0 — establish trust before breadth

1. Write a short “spectral contract” reference document from the equations,
   covering the estimand, one-sided folding, CSD orientation, detrend,
   missingness, and boundaries.
2. Add the independent direct-DFT oracle under tests or benchmarks.
3. Test `vectorized_ltf_plan`, including parity and documented differences
   from `ltf_plan`.
4. Replace silent input/output `nan_to_num` behavior with a designed policy.
5. Finish the already-started pointwise PSD confidence interval, while
   labeling its assumptions and boundary limitations.

Stop after this phase if the current estimator does not pass its own stated
contract. Fix the estimator or narrow the claim before adding features.

### Phase 1 — expose the measurement

1. Design `SpectralPlan` and its compatibility view.
2. Implement numerical spectral-response evaluation for a single plan row.
3. Add plan reporting and stable hashing.
4. Add optional replicate retention to the direct oracle first, then to one
   production path.
5. Build the synthetic and real benchmark corpus.

### Phase 2 — run research prototypes

Prototype outside the public API:

1. response-aware scheduler optimization;
2. debiased dynamic WOSA;
3. dynamic multitaper with jackknife or derived covariance;
4. robust replicate reduction.

Predeclare benchmark cases and metrics before comparing them. Select at most
one or two methods for production.

### Phase 3 — make multichannel analysis native

1. Implement shared Fourier vectors and spectral matrices on one plan.
2. Preserve exact one- and two-channel compatibility.
3. Add matrix whitening, multiple/partial coherence, and MISO residuals as
   matrix reductions.
4. Add shrinkage and uncertainty only after replicate counts and conditioning
   are explicit.

### Phase 4 — handle real data honestly

1. Add validity masks and compare direct missing-data PSD estimation with
   conditional filling.
2. Add stationarity diagnostics and replicate traces.
3. Add inference-aware continuum/line models.
4. Add calibrated delay and projection utilities from the existing handoffs.

### Phase 5 — publication and ecosystem

1. Publish the estimator contract and benchmark corpus with the method paper.
2. Add optional xarray, Astropy/Pint, and GWpy adapters.
3. Add bounded-memory execution and then evaluate CPU batching versus GPU.
4. Maintain a numerical changelog for every estimator behavior change.

## Settled decisions

- LTPDA feature parity is not the organizing goal.
- The existing ten handoffs remain; do not delete or rewrite their history.
- LTPDA output is not automatically ground truth.
- Bias, variance, resolution, and covariance belong in one estimator design.
- The default scientific policy for missing samples must not be silent zero
  replacement.
- A small reproducibility record is required even though a full LTPDA
  provenance graph is not.
- The summary-statistic path remains important for speed, but an optional
  replicate path is required for advanced inference.
- N-channel work must produce a shared spectral matrix by construction.
- Unit strings are transitional metadata, not a complete unit system.
- GPU work waits for an estimator and benchmark that justify it.
- No method becomes public because it is sophisticated. It becomes public
  because its contract is clear and it improves a declared use case.

## What not to do

- Do not implement the ten handoffs as ten isolated public functions and call
  the library complete.
- Do not report narrower variance bars without measuring leakage bias.
- Do not describe `M2` as assumption-free uncertainty of the mean.
- Do not infer independent frequency bins merely because
  `f[j+1] - f[j]` is approximately `r[j]`.
- Do not fill gaps and then treat reconstructed samples as observations.
- Do not fit a continuum while ignoring the estimator response and then use
  residuals for line significance.
- Do not use `pinv` as the only answer to an underdetermined spectral matrix.
- Do not optimize only wall time.
- Do not port LTPDA inconsistencies for behavioral parity.
- Do not broaden required dependencies to obtain metadata or adapters.
